Sample: project understanding — an opening

Every bidder has to write Section IIC-5, and it starts with understanding. Here is how we would open it. Firm facts are placeholders; the project facts are from the RFP and its Exhibit A, the Regional Parks Funding Task Team Report (May 2026).

Clark County is not asking for a campaign. It is asking for public education — a disciplined, factual account of why a park system with more than 100 parks and 7,166 acres of parkland is being maintained by 37 operations and maintenance staff, why a third of daily maintenance tasks go uncompleted, and why the county has drawn on reserves for seven consecutive years to keep its regional parks open. [FIRM] understands the distinction and would hold it: every deliverable in this engagement must inform residents about needs, funding gaps and options, and stand up to scrutiny as education rather than advocacy, because the credibility of the November 2027 decision depends on it.

The Task Team report gives the education effort its raw material, and it is strong material. The Greater Clark Parks District, approved in 2005, funds parks only inside Vancouver's unincorporated urban growth area; eight of the county's most heavily used regional parks — Lewisville, Daybreak, Lucia Falls, Moulton Falls, Lacamas, Whipple Creek, Vancouver Lake and Frenchman's Bar — sit outside it, reliant on a general fund that covers about 23% of operating costs and park revenue that covers about 12%. Two funding models are on the table: a county-wide expansion of the metropolitan park district, generating an estimated $15 million a year at roughly today's 13-cent rate, or a six-year regional parks levy under RCW 36.68.525 requiring 60% voter approval. Each carries different equity questions — the report itself flags the layered-tax concern for GCPD residents — and honest materials will present both models' trade-offs plainly.

[FIRM]'s approach follows the county's own phasing. From November 2026 through January 2027 we would build the communication strategy and message architecture, writing to the branding package the county's Public Information Office will supply. When the separately retained research firm fields its first survey in December 2026, we translate — turning price-sensitivity, values and timing findings into message testing and engagement priorities in the February–March 2027 integration window. From April 2027 the work becomes production: presentations, talking points, fact sheets, FAQs and digital content, delivered in accessible, county-compatible formats for the county to finalize and distribute. Stakeholder engagement runs the whole length of the contract, building on the coordination county staff have already begun with the cities and community organizations named in the report.
