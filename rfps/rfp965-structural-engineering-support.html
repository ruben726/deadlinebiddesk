Sample: project approach and understanding — an opening

Every bidder has to write Section IIC-5 inside a four-page limit, so it has to work hard. Here is how we would open it. Firm facts are placeholders; the project facts are from the RFP.

An on-call structural contract is judged by a different clock than a capital project. Clark County's bridge and structure programs will hand [FIRM] task assignments that arrive without warning — a scour question after a storm, a load rating for an overweight haul, an emergency inspection — alongside planned work like TS&L development, retrofit feasibility studies and grant applications. The RFP is explicit that there is no guaranteed expenditure and that every task's scope, schedule and fee is fixed in writing before work begins; our approach is built around making those task assignments fast to scope, cheap to administer and predictable to close out. For each request, [FIRM] returns a short written scope with assumptions, deliverables, schedule and a not-to-exceed fee within [N] business days, so the county's contract administrator can issue the assignment — or redirect us — without burning program time.

The technical breadth the county lists is the breadth we would staff. Our certified bridge inspector and proposed team leader, [NAME], holds [CERTIFICATION DETAILS] and meets 23 CFR 650.305; behind that role sit engineers with [WSDOT/AASHTO LRFD design, load rating using WSDOT and AASHTO procedures, and hydraulic analysis] experience, with specialty access — underwater and rope access — provided by [TEAM MEMBER OR SUBCONSULTANT]. Scour evaluation and countermeasure design follow HEC-18 and HEC-23, as the RFP specifies, and inspection deliverables follow WSDOT inspection procedures and reporting so that county records stay consistent with the state inventory.

Because most tasks begin with what the county already knows, we start every assignment by working from the county's own file — as-built plans, inspection reports, existing load ratings and scour appraisals — before proposing new field work. That discipline keeps small tasks small: a maintenance evaluation should not grow into a design contract unless the structure genuinely warrants it, and when it does, our concept plans and cost estimates are written so the county can carry them straight into a grant application. On budget control, our record is simple to check: on [REFERENCE CONTRACT], [N] task assignments were delivered over [PERIOD] with [OUTCOME — e.g., every task closed at or under its written fee]; the reference contacts in Section [X] will confirm it.

[FIRM] treats a four-year, task-order contract as a standing promise of availability. Our commitment: an acknowledged response to any county request within [N] hours, and honest advice when a task is better done by county staff.
