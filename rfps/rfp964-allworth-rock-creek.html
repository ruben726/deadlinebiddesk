Evaluation breakdown

Two-tier, 200 points total in the event interviews are performed (Section IIIA-2, PDF p.18). All received proposals are scored on Tier One, multiplied by .40; top scorers are invited to interview and scored on Tier Two, multiplied by .60. The county intends to interview a minimum of three teams if enough proposals arrive (Section IIIA-1, PDF p.18; confirmed in Q&A #6).

Tier One — written proposal (100 points, weighted ×0.40)

Sample: project approach and understanding — an opening

Every bidder has to write Section IIC-5. Here is how we would open it. Firm facts are placeholders; the project facts are from the RFP and the county's published Q&A.

Rock Creek has outgrown the two corrugated metal pipes that carry NE Allworth Road across it. The RFP is candid about their condition: the 60-inch and 36-inch CMPs are heavily corroded along their bottoms, the roadway embankment shows severe scour, and two transverse depressions are visible in the road surface — evidence that the crossing is failing from below. [FIRM] reads this as two problems that must be solved as one: a structural replacement sized for the 100-year hydrologic condition, and a fish-passage barrier correction designed to WDFW's Stream Simulation guidelines. The 30% alternatives analysis is where those two demands either reconcile or collide, and we treat it as the project's critical decision point rather than a formality: each alternative will carry an aerial layout, a defensible cost estimate built from current county bid tabs, and an explicit statement of its permitting and right-of-way consequences, so the County can choose a structure knowing what it commits to downstream.

The county's Q&A responses sharpen the picture. There are no existing hydraulic or hydrologic models for the site, no bathymetric survey in the County's scope, and no geotechnical or structural data for the existing culverts. Early field data collection therefore sits on the critical path: [FIRM] would mobilize geotechnical exploration and hydraulic/geomorphic field work immediately after the kick-off meeting, so that the December 2026 – March 2027 survey window the County has reserved for its own topographic work is matched, not followed, by ours.

Permitting drives the schedule. The 60% Permit Set must contain everything the County needs to pursue the HPA, the Corps Nationwide Permit with Ecology's water quality certification, and the County's own floodplain and wetland/habitat permits — and the published schedule then allows a full year for the permitting process. Because the project carries no federal funds, the County will not prepare a NEPA document; but our Section 106 cultural resources survey and ESA Section 7 documentation must still stand up to the Corps' own compliance review, which shapes how we scope and write them from day one. Right-of-way follows the 90% set, so encroachment and easement questions get settled in the post-30% window the RFP reserves for exactly that discussion.

[FIRM] would deliver this with [PM NAME] as project manager and a Washington-licensed engineer stamping every submittal, supported by [SUBCONSULTANT ROLES]. Our commitment to the voluntary 10% SBE goal is set out in Section [X], and our approach to the County's review cadence — five design review milestones, comment resolution logged and closed in writing — in Section [Y].
